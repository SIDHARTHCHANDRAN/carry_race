The Ripple vs Look-Ahead Carry Race

Every addition your computer does depends on carries. A ripple-carry adder waits for each carry in turn; a carry look-ahead adder (CLA) works them all out in advance. Pick two numbers and watch both circuits race, one gate delay at a time.

How much faster as numbers get bigger?

Gate delays until the last sum bit is ready, using the textbook model (ripple: 2n−1, look-ahead: 4).

Real chips cap the fan-in of gates, so very wide adders use look-ahead in groups of 4 bits. The idea is the same: do not wait, predict.

The idea in 3 lines

Gi = xi·yi  (Generate: makes a carry itself)
Pi = xi+yi  (Propagate: passes a carry on)
ci+1 = Gi + Pi·ci
si = xi ⊕ yi ⊕ ci

G and P use only x and y, so every bit has them after just 1 gate delay.

Why it is fast: unroll the carries

c₁ = G₀ + P₀c₀
c₂ = G₁ + P₁G₀ + P₁P₀c₀
c₃ = G₂ + P₂G₁ + P₂P₁G₀ + P₂P₁P₀c₀
c₄ = G₃ + P₃G₂ + P₃P₂G₁ + P₃P₂P₁G₀ + P₃P₂P₁P₀c₀

Each line is an AND-OR circuit: 3 gate delays, no waiting on the previous carry.
